I might die tomorrow. And I fear the uncertainty that I never will know.

I remember as a kid, my parents used to didactically reinforce the importance of hard work and saving. Anything that lacked foresight would be met with a naggy yet endearing - Why did you waste so much money buying this? Why didn't you study harder? Why didn't you put in more effort into learning this...?

As a kid, I never once questioned it. The idea of death and a permanent departure from the world is a morbid idea that should not weigh on the minds of the youth. And given that humans are inherently reactionary - we are agents that react to stimulus and evolutionarily so - these thoughts must have come from somewhere, an experience that triggers the idea that - oh hey one day I will be gone from this place forever. 

It could be the realisation of the seemingly slow, yet unyielding passing of time that slowly chips away at our mortality. It could also be the experience of loss of a person we hold near and dear, when we realise that we too and the people around us are not exempt from the shackles and limitations of being human.
